How to Choose a Surgeon

📚 Deeper reading. This is one of the detailed research pages. You don't need it to get ready. Start with Sarah's home page and What to know.

Medical disclaimer: This private guide is general education compiled from published sources and the public website of Dr. Rovner's practice. It is not medical advice and does not replace the instructions Sarah gets from her surgical team. If anything here differs from what they say, follow them. For trouble breathing or rapidly increasing neck swelling, call 911 (see red-flag card).

Results in trials come from experienced, selected surgeons and carefully selected patients [11]. Finding a surgeon who does this operation regularly and will tell you honestly whether you are a candidate is one of the most important decisions you make.

Training and specialty

  • Cervical disc replacement is performed by orthopaedic surgeons and neurosurgeons [29]. Ask whether your surgeon is board-certified and has completed spine fellowship training.
  • Expert consensus statements on return to activity and on athletes were written by spine surgeons with high volumes of disc replacement (for example, 10 experts averaging 15 years in practice) [71, 72]. Ask your surgeon how their own practice compares.

Experience and volume

  • Ask how many cervical disc replacements, and specifically two-level procedures, the surgeon performs each year.
  • Ask about their personal rates of reoperation, dysphagia and hoarseness, and how they compare with published ranges [58, 66, 52].
  • Ask whether they also perform ACDF and posterior foraminotomy. A surgeon who offers all three can recommend the best fit rather than the only option [27].

Device choice and transparency

  • A good surgeon can explain why they prefer a device, its FDA indication for one or two levels, its materials and MRI behavior, and any safety alerts [10, 39, 20].
  • Be cautious of cross-trial claims like "our device had the highest success rate". Trials used different control groups [5, 18, 41].
  • Ask about industry relationships. Many trial investigators disclose consulting or royalty relationships with manufacturers [42].

Signs of a careful evaluation

  • They examine strength, sensation and reflexes, and match them to your MRI [26, 32]
  • They review flexion-extension X-rays and, when needed, CT for facet arthritis and spurs [23]
  • They check bone health where relevant [22, 18]
  • They explain nonsurgical options and natural history first [26, 36]
  • They tell you when you are not a candidate for disc replacement [25]

Second opinions

Getting a second opinion is reasonable for any elective spine surgery, especially when you are deciding between disc replacement, fusion or foraminotomy, or between one and two levels. Bring your imaging discs and reports, not only the radiology summary.

Hospital or surgery center

  • Many disc replacements are now done with a short stay; in the two-level Simplify study, mean hospital stay was 0.59 days [18].
  • Ask whether the facility is in-network, and who you call after hours if you have trouble swallowing or breathing [29, 27].
