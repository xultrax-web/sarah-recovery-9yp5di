Costs, Insurance and Prior Authorization

📚 Deeper reading. This is one of the detailed research pages. You don't need it to get ready. Start with Sarah's home page and What to know.

Medical disclaimer: This private guide is general education compiled from published sources and the public website of Dr. Rovner's practice. It is not medical advice and does not replace the instructions Sarah gets from her surgical team. If anything here differs from what they say, follow them. For trouble breathing or rapidly increasing neck swelling, call 911 (see red-flag card).

Out-of-pocket cost depends on your plan, the facility (hospital vs outpatient surgery center), the surgeon and the device. Published figures are model estimates, not prices. The steps below focus on what insurers actually require.

What published data say about cost

  • A Markov model based on the two-level Prestige trial estimated 5-year payer costs of $130,417 for two-level disc replacement and $116,717 for two-level ACDF. Disc replacement produced slightly more quality-adjusted life years (3.45 vs 3.23). The ICER was $62,337 per QALY, and both were judged cost-effective [24].
  • These are payer costs from a model, not what a patient pays. Ask your surgeon's office and facility for a written estimate.

Medicare

  • There is no national coverage determination for cervical disc replacement. Coverage is set by regional contractors [21].
  • LCD L38033 (Palmetto GBA) applies in Alabama, Georgia, Tennessee, South Carolina, Virginia, West Virginia and North Carolina [21].
  • It requires at least 6 weeks of failed conservative care including physician-directed pain management (unless symptoms are severe or progressive) and imaging-confirmed compression at C3–C7. Two-level surgery must use a two-level-approved device [21].
  • It lists as investigational: hybrid ADR + fusion, prior surgery at the level, prior fusion at another level, moderate-to-severe facet arthropathy, 3 or more levels, and non-contiguous levels [21].

Commercial insurance (Aetna example)

  • Device-specific age ranges apply (e.g. Mobi-C 21–67 for one level; Prestige LP 21–78) (full table) [22].
  • Requires 6 weeks of in-person physical therapy within the past year, waived for cord compression, myelopathy, severe weakness (4− or worse) or progressive deficit [22].
  • No diagnosis of osteoporosis or osteopenia; for people aged 60 or older, a DEXA T-score of −1.0 or better within the past year. For people with diabetes, HbA1c below 8% within 3 months before surgery, unless there is an urgent indication such as myelopathy [22].
  • Hybrid ADR + fusion is considered medically necessary. More than two levels and the M6-C are considered experimental [22].

Prior authorization checklist

Most of these items come straight from the policy criteria above. Gather them with your surgeon's office:

  • Diagnosis: radiculopathy and/or myelopathy at specific level(s), with exam findings (strength, sensation, reflexes) [21, 22]
  • How symptoms limit your daily activities, and that other causes of pain were ruled out [22]
  • Imaging report (MRI or CT) showing compression that matches your symptoms [21]
  • Dates and type of conservative care within the past year: in-person PT for the full 6 weeks, medications (e.g. NSAIDs, acetaminophen), injections. A PT discharge note documenting lack of benefit can count as a waiver for Aetna [22, 21]
  • Documentation of any exception: progressive weakness, myelopathy, cord compression [22]
  • DEXA result if you are 60 or older (Aetna), and HbA1c within 3 months if you have diabetes [22]
  • The specific device and whether it is FDA-approved for your number of levels [21, 22]
  • CPT codes (22856 ± 22858) and facility type [22]

If you are denied: ask for the written reason and the exact policy used, check whether a criterion (e.g. PT duration or DEXA) was simply missing from the paperwork, and ask your surgeon about a peer-to-peer review or a formal appeal. These are general steps; your plan's documents describe the actual process.
