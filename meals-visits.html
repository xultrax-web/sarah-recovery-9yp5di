Thursday, October 8, 2026Surgery day

Visiting tips

  • Short and sweet: 20–30 minutes, and please text before you come. (Our family's request.)
  • No big hugs, nothing that pulls on her neck or shoulders. A hand squeeze is perfect. Her neck needs to avoid strain and twisting while it heals [91, 76].
  • Stay home if you're sick. A cold or cough is not what she needs right now. (Our family's request.)
  • She may be quiet or hoarse. Talking can be tiring, and hoarseness is common after this surgery [27, 77].
  • Help with a task rather than bringing gifts: take out the trash, fold laundry, walk the dog, or join her for a gentle walk. Lifting and household chores are off-limits for her for a while [91].

Soft-food meal ideas

Her throat may be sore and swallowing may be uncomfortable for a week or two after surgery [77, 25]. Soft, moist foods are easiest [90, 93]:

  • Soups and stews (not too hot), chicken and rice soup, lentil soup
  • Mac and cheese, soft pasta with sauce, shepherd's pie
  • Mashed potatoes with gravy, soft-cooked vegetables
  • Scrambled eggs, egg dishes, cottage cheese
  • Oatmeal or cream of wheat, soft muffins
  • Pudding, custard, applesauce, smoothies, popsicles

Food ideas follow the VA swallowing handout and Norton's post-surgery advice [90, 93]. Disposable containers mean no dishes to return. Please label the food and any allergens. Wesley also appreciates a meal on surgery day.
