Am I a Candidate? Indications and Contraindications

📚 Deeper reading. This is one of the detailed research pages. You don't need it to get ready. Start with Sarah's home page and What to know.

Medical disclaimer: This private guide is general education compiled from published sources and the public website of Dr. Rovner's practice. It is not medical advice and does not replace the instructions Sarah gets from her surgical team. If anything here differs from what they say, follow them. For trouble breathing or rapidly increasing neck swelling, call 911 (see red-flag card).

Whether you are a good candidate depends on your symptoms, imaging, bone quality, and the specific device label and insurance policy. The lists below come straight from the FDA label summary for one device, the NASS recommendation, the Medicare LCD and Aetna. They overlap, but not perfectly.

Typical good-candidate profile

  • Arm pain, numbness or weakness (radiculopathy) and/or myelopathy from one or two discs between C3 and C7 [23, 25]
  • Imaging shows a disc herniation or spondylosis compressing the root or cord at the level(s) matching your symptoms [21]
  • At least 6 weeks of failed conservative care, or progressive or severe neurological deficit [23, 21, 9]
  • No significant facet-joint arthritis or bony compression of the nerves [25]
  • Skeletally mature adult. Each device has its own age range in coverage policy (see below) [22]

Where candidacy rules differ

  • Instability threshold: 3.0 mm (Simplify label), 3 mm (NASS) or 3.5 mm (Medicare LCD), all with 11° [18, 23, 21].
  • Bone density: T-score below −1.5 excludes under the Simplify label [18]. Aetna requires a DEXA T-score of −1.0 or better within the past year for people 60 or older [22]. The Medicare LCD excludes any osteopenia [21].
  • Hybrid surgery (disc replacement at one level plus fusion at another): NASS says it "can be considered" and Aetna considers it medically necessary, but the Palmetto Medicare LCD lists it as investigational [23, 22, 21].
  • Prior neck surgery: the LCD treats prior surgery at the treated level, or a prior fusion at another level, as investigational [21]. The Mobi-C trial excluded both [11].
  • Diabetes: Aetna requires HbA1c below 8% for people with diabetes [22].
  • Smoking: heavy smokers (>1 pack/day) were excluded from the Mobi-C trial [11]. A 109-patient study found similar outcomes in smokers [40], but Cleveland Clinic may ask you to quit at least 4 weeks before surgery because nicotine interferes with healing [29], and AAOS says smoking negatively affects surgical success and recovery [25].

Aetna also requires that other sources of pain have been ruled out, that symptoms limit daily activities, that imaging shows compression (or at least moderate stenosis) at the matching level, and that there is no segmental instability [22].

Source: Aetna CPB 0591 [22]. Other insurers use different rules; ask your surgeon's office for your plan's policy.

Myelopathy and athletes

FDA labels include myelopathy for several devices [9, 18]. In a surgeon Delphi consensus on high-impact athletes, there was agreement that disc replacement was acceptable for radiculopathy (74%) but not for myelopathy (47%) [72].
