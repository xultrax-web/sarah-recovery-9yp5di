Two-Level Disc Replacement (C5–C6 + C6–C7)

📚 Deeper reading. This is one of the detailed research pages. You don't need it to get ready. Start with Sarah's home page and What to know.

Medical disclaimer: This private guide is general education compiled from published sources and the public website of Dr. Rovner's practice. It is not medical advice and does not replace the instructions Sarah gets from her surgical team. If anything here differs from what they say, follow them. For trouble breathing or rapidly increasing neck swelling, call 911 (see red-flag card).

Many patients have problems at both C5–C6 and C6–C7. In the two-level Mobi-C trial, C5–C7 accounted for 72.9% of disc-replacement patients [11]. In the two-level Simplify study, 73% were treated at C5/6–C6/7 [18].

Devices approved for two contiguous levels

DeviceTwo-level approvalKey two-level evidence
Mobi-C08/23/2013 [10]RCT 225 vs 105. 24-month success 69.7% vs 37.4% [11]; reoperation 3.1% vs 11.4% [41]. At 7 years success 60.8% vs 34.2%, index reoperation 4.4% vs 16.2% [42]
Prestige LP07/07/2016 [13]RCT 209 vs 188. At 10 years success 80.4% vs 62.2%; index reoperation 4.7% vs 17.6% [44]
Simplify04/01/2021 [17]182 patients, 86.7% overall success at 2 years; historical ACDF controls [17, 18]
prodisc C Vivo / SK10/10/2025 [4]433 analyzed; 87.1% vs 83.7% composite success, compared with another FDA-approved disc [5]

Not two-level approved: Bryan, Secure-C and the original Prodisc-C (one level) [6, 7, 2]. M6-C was one level only, and the UK alert notes multi-level use was removed from its indications [15, 20]. The Medicare LCD covers two-level surgery only with a device approved for two levels [21].

Why two levels may favor disc replacement

  • In the Mobi-C trial, the advantage of disc replacement was greater at two levels than at one. Two-level ADR was superior at 7 years, while one-level ADR was non-inferior [42].
  • Two-level fusion in the Mobi-C trial had a 12.4% nonunion rate at 24 months [11].
  • A two-level meta-analysis (8 studies) favored ADR for success, secondary surgery and complications [55].
  • Two-level adjacent-segment reoperation had about 49% lower odds with ADR [53].
  • Two-level Mobi-C kept a mean 10.1° (upper) and 8.3° (lower) of flexion-extension at 24 months [11].
  • In claims data, multilevel ACDF patients in particular had more complications and revisions than ADR patients [60].

Two-level concerns

  • HO at two levels: 10-year grade IV HO was 8.2% (upper) and 10.3% (lower), and grade III–IV about 39% [44].
  • Swallowing and voice: dysphagia/dysphonia was reported in 16.7% after two-level Mobi-C vs 22.9% after two-level ACDF through 24 months [11].
  • Sport: only 23.5% of expert surgeons would clear an athlete for high-impact, high-frequency sport after two-level ADR [72].
  • Cost: a 5-year payer model found two-level ADR cost $130,417 vs $116,717 for ACDF but produced more QALYs (3.45 vs 3.23). The ICER was $62,337 per QALY, and the authors concluded both are cost-effective [24].

Hybrid surgery (one disc + one fusion)

Sometimes one level suits a disc (for example C5–C6) but the other doesn't (for example a collapsed C6–C7 with spurs). The options are then a hybrid construct or a two-level fusion. Coverage differs: NASS says hybrid "can be considered" and Aetna considers it medically necessary, but the Palmetto Medicare LCD lists it as investigational [23, 22, 21]. An expert panel agreed that patients may return to physical activity earlier after 2- or 3-level ADR than after hybrid constructs [71].

Three or more levels

Not FDA-approved for any listed device. Not supported by NASS, investigational under the Medicare LCD, and experimental under Aetna [23, 21, 22].

Questions specific to two-level surgery

  • Are both levels causing my symptoms, or only one? How do you know (exam, MRI, EMG)? [32, 26]
  • Do both discs meet the criteria for a disc (disc height, facets, spurs)? [23]
  • Which two-level-approved device do you use, and why? [21]
  • Would you recommend a hybrid, and will my insurer cover it? [22, 21]
