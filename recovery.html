Recovery Week by Week: Work, Driving and Sports

Medical disclaimer: This private guide is general education compiled from published sources and the public website of Dr. Rovner's practice. It is not medical advice and does not replace the instructions Sarah gets from her surgical team. If anything here differs from what they say, follow them. For trouble breathing or rapidly increasing neck swelling, call 911 (see red-flag card).

Recovery after disc replacement is usually faster than after fusion because there is no fusion to wait for. Timelines still vary by person, by job, and by surgeon. Scroll to animate the timeline. Each step shows where the guidance comes from, and sources disagree slightly.

Day of surgery – day 1

Hospital or same-day discharge

Most people stay 1–2 days; some go home the same day [25]. Walking and eating usually start on the first day [27]. Someone else must drive you home [29].

Day 2

Daily self-care

Activities of daily living such as grooming and preparing meals usually resume by the second day [29]. Swallowing may feel difficult for a few days [25].

Weeks 1–2

Desk work, driving, travel

AAOS: most patients return to driving and desk jobs within 1–2 weeks [25]. An expert panel agreed patients may return to walking, social activities, sedentary work, air travel and sexual activity within 2 weeks [71]. Your surgeon decides when driving is safe for you.

Weeks 2–3

Regular light activities

Cleveland Clinic: light activities between 2 and 3 weeks [29]. Avoid heavy lifting and aerobic exercise until cleared [29].

Weeks 4–6

Light exercise, physical therapy, most activities

Light physical activity at 4–6 weeks (expert consensus) [71]. Full activities except contact sports by 4–6 weeks [29]. Range-of-motion and strengthening exercises often begin around 4–6 weeks after anterior neck surgery, sometimes with PT [27].

About 6 weeks

Training and heavier work

The expert panel agreed on intense conditioning and sport-specific training at 6 weeks, but had only weaker agreement on physically demanding work at 4–6 weeks and high-intensity sport at 6 weeks [71]. In the Bryan trial, 49.2% of disc-replacement patients were back at work at 6 weeks vs 39.4% after fusion [49].

Weeks 6–12

Strenuous jobs

AAOS: returning to strenuous jobs usually takes 6–12 weeks [25]. Mean return to work in the two-level Mobi-C trial was 45.9 days vs 66.8 days after fusion [11].

About 3 months

Contact and collision sports (selected athletes)

In a 34-surgeon consensus, the most endorsed timelines after one-level replacement were 6 weeks or 3 months for practice and 3 months for games. Only 23.5% would let an athlete return to high-impact, high-frequency sport after a two-level replacement, compared with 57.6% after one level [72].

Up to 6 months

Full recovery

It may take up to 6 months to recover fully [29]. Improvements in pain and disability come over weeks to months, and disc replacement improves but does not completely eliminate pain [25].

1 year and beyond

Follow-up imaging

Follow-up visits check that the disc is working [29]. Work outcomes: disc-replacement patients had higher odds of working at 6 weeks, 3 months and 1 year, but not at 2 years or later [57]. People with an M6-C should have annual X-rays according to the UK MHRA [20].

Return to work: the evidence

  • Meta-analysis of 16 studies (5657 patients): higher odds of working at 6 weeks (OR 1.33), 3 months (OR 1.58) and 1 year (OR 1.35); about 9.9 days earlier on average; no difference at 2 years or later [57].
  • Bryan trial: more patients back at 6 weeks, no difference by 6 months [49].

Driving

AAOS says most people can drive within 1–2 weeks [25]. Ask your surgeon when you may drive, especially if you are still taking prescription pain medicine or wearing a collar.

Sports

  • General patients: full activity except contact sports by 4–6 weeks [29]; sport-specific training at 6 weeks (expert consensus) [71].
  • High-impact athletes: much less agreement, and more caution after two-level surgery [72].

Where recovery advice differs

  • Light activities: "1–2 weeks" for desk work and driving (AAOS) vs "2–3 weeks" for regular light activities (Cleveland Clinic) [25, 29].
  • Full recovery: "full activities excluding contact sports by 4–6 weeks" vs "up to six months" for full recovery, both from Cleveland Clinic. These describe different milestones [29].
  • Strenuous work: 6–12 weeks (AAOS) vs only weak expert consensus for 4–6 weeks [25, 71].
