Design and materials (where documented)

  • General: implants aim to reproduce the size and function of a normal disc. Materials include medical-grade polyethylene and cobalt-chromium or titanium alloys [25]. Cleveland Clinic describes the main material types as cobalt, titanium and stainless steel [29]. The two lists differ, and newer designs also use PEEK and ceramics [18].
  • Simplify: PEEK endplates with a titanium coating and a zirconia-toughened alumina (ZTA) ceramic core. MR Conditional at 1.5 T and 3 T, with image artifact extending about 5 mm (1.5 T) and 8 mm (3 T) from the device [18].
  • M6-C: a compressible viscoelastic nuclear core with an annular structure [51]. Wear debris from its ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene has been linked to osteolysis [67].
  • prodisc C Vivo / SK: two versions, marketed by the manufacturer as "Match-the-Disc" devices [5].
  • Mobi-C: trial exclusions included allergy to cobalt, chromium, molybdenum or polyethylene, which indicates the materials used [11].

MRI after disc replacement

If you may need future neck MRIs, ask about artifact. In a comparison of implant materials, titanium caused the least artifact. Cobalt-chrome obscured the treated level, and stainless steel obscured both the treated and neighboring levels [39]. Always tell MRI staff about your implant and bring the device card.

Safety alerts and device-specific concerns

M6-C (Spinal Kinetics)

  • The UK MHRA alert (15 January 2026) recommends annual X-rays for patients with an M6-C because of osteolysis risk. It reports that the manufacturer discontinued the device in February 2025 and that multi-level use was removed from the indications [20].
  • In a single-center series, 18 of 53 M6-C patients (34%) were revised at an average of 67 months for wear-induced osteolysis, which was often asymptomatic. Many of these were multi-level or hybrid cases [67]. Another series (43 of 85 patients followed, mean 8.1 years) found implant failure with severe osteolysis in 12% and osteolysis around 44% of implants [68].
  • In contrast, the 5-year IDE study reported 82.3% composite success vs 67.0% for matched fusion controls and subsequent surgery of 3.1% vs 5.3% [51]. See disagreements.
  • Aetna lists the M6-C as experimental/investigational [22].

Simplify

A 2026 review of 96 FDA MAUDE adverse-event reports and revision specimens described PEEK wear, titanium-coating delamination and osteolysis. The authors call for cautious patient selection until durability is confirmed [69]. A 2025 MAUDE analysis found that osteolysis appeared in 36.2% of valid reports for M6 (138 events) and 25.8% for Simplify (16 events). Every other device had 0–3 osteolysis reports [70]. Reporting to MAUDE is optional for surgeons, so these are not true incidence rates [70].

Comparing devices: a caution

Success rates from different pivotal trials cannot be compared head-to-head. Mobi-C and Prestige LP were compared with randomized fusion controls [41, 44]. Simplify and M6-C used non-randomized historical or propensity-matched fusion controls [18, 51]. The two-level prodisc C Vivo/SK trial compared against another artificial disc [5]. Most trials were sponsored by the manufacturers [42].
