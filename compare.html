Disc Replacement vs Fusion vs Posterior Foraminotomy

📚 Deeper reading. This is one of the detailed research pages. You don't need it to get ready. Start with Sarah's home page and What to know.

Medical disclaimer: This private guide is general education compiled from published sources and the public website of Dr. Rovner's practice. It is not medical advice and does not replace the instructions Sarah gets from her surgical team. If anything here differs from what they say, follow them. For trouble breathing or rapidly increasing neck swelling, call 911 (see red-flag card).

Three operations are commonly used for cervical radiculopathy: anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF), artificial disc replacement (ADR), and posterior cervical (lamino)foraminotomy (PCF) [27]. Each relieves pressure on the nerve. They differ in approach and in what happens to motion at the treated level.

What the long-term randomized evidence says

  • Repeat operations: in a meta-analysis of 11 multicenter RCTs, adjacent-level surgery rates were similar at 2 years. A difference favoring disc replacement emerged around 5 years. Index-level reoperation was lower with disc replacement at every time point [52].
  • Level-matched analysis (2026): one-level ADR had about 55% lower odds of reoperation for adjacent-segment disease (OR 0.45); two-level ADR had 49% lower odds (OR 0.51); mean follow-up 7 years [53].
  • Symptoms at 10 years: disability (NDI) and pain (VAS) scores were statistically better with ADR, but the differences did not reach the minimal clinically important difference. JOA scores (myelopathy) were lower with ADR, and neurological success did not differ [54].
  • Overall: a 14-RCT meta-analysis (3160 patients) found disc replacement superior on several outcomes but noted continuing uncertainty about long-term prosthesis degeneration [56].

Why surgeons may still recommend fusion

  • Your anatomy may rule out ADR: facet arthritis, instability, a very collapsed disc, bridging spurs, osteoporosis or deformity [23, 18].
  • Three or more levels are not covered and are considered experimental [23, 21, 22].
  • Insurance: age limits and other criteria apply, such as device-specific age ranges in Aetna's policy [22].
  • ADR has its own risks: heterotopic ossification, osteolysis with some designs, and implant-related revision [66, 20].

Why foraminotomy may be offered

For one-sided nerve-root compression, a posterior foraminotomy avoids the front-of-neck approach and usually avoids fusion [27]. The 2024 meta-analysis favored minimally invasive foraminotomy, but called its findings preliminary and in need of longer, larger studies [59].
