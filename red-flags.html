Red-Flag Card: When to Call for Help

Medical disclaimer: This private guide is general education compiled from published sources and the public website of Dr. Rovner's practice. It is not medical advice and does not replace the instructions Sarah gets from her surgical team. If anything here differs from what they say, follow them. For trouble breathing or rapidly increasing neck swelling, call 911 (see red-flag card).

Print this card and keep it on the fridge. When in doubt, call. No one on the team minds a call. The categories below come from published instructions written for front-of-neck spine surgery. Your surgeon's instructions take priority.

Call 911 or go to the ER now

  • Any trouble breathing, or a feeling that your breathing has changed. Swallowing trouble can be normal; breathing trouble never is [92, 93, 27].
  • Neck swelling that grows quickly or is large, especially with trouble breathing [93, 94].
  • Chest pain or sudden shortness of breath [93, 91, 88].
  • Significant new weakness, or being unable to fully move your arms or legs [93, 94].
  • Loss of control of bowels or bladder [94].

Call the surgeon's office right away

Office: (925) 275-0700After hours: To fill in: after-hours number
  • Fever: over 101°F that persists, per the practice [76]. MedlinePlus says chills or fever of 101°F or higher [88]. Ask the team for their exact number.
  • Swallowing that is getting worse or not improving, trouble eating or drinking, or coughing when you swallow [29, 89].
  • Incision: continuous bleeding; yellow or green drainage; more redness, swelling or warmth; or the edges opening [29, 88].
  • New numbness, tingling or weakness, or a change from how you felt before surgery [29, 88, 94].
  • Severe pain that your medicine doesn't control [29].
  • Calf pain or swelling, which can be a sign of a blood clot [88, 91].
  • Trouble passing urine, or a severe headache that won't go away with usual medicine [88, 94].

Usually expected (mention at your follow-up)

  • A sore throat for several days, and some swallowing difficulty for days to weeks, especially with dry food or big pills [92, 27, 94].
  • Hoarseness that slowly improves [27, 77].
  • Aching at the back of the neck and between the shoulder blades [93, 94].
  • Some bruising and swelling near the incision, and a little drainage at first [92, 76].
  • A low fever in the first few days, which the practice describes as normal (see the note about their wording on Your care team) [76].

Hospital and practice handouts list slightly different symptoms and thresholds. This card combines them. A doctor would treat breathing trouble and fast-growing neck swelling as the most urgent.
