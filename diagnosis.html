Diagnosis and Imaging

📚 Deeper reading. This is one of the detailed research pages. You don't need it to get ready. Start with Sarah's home page and What to know.

Medical disclaimer: This private guide is general education compiled from published sources and the public website of Dr. Rovner's practice. It is not medical advice and does not replace the instructions Sarah gets from her surgical team. If anything here differs from what they say, follow them. For trouble breathing or rapidly increasing neck swelling, call 911 (see red-flag card).

The goal is to prove which level and which structure is causing your symptoms. That matters even more for disc replacement, because the implant only suits certain kinds of degeneration.

History and physical examination

Your doctor will examine your neck, shoulders, arms and hands, checking for muscle weakness, loss of sensation and reflex changes. They may ask you to do neck and arm movements that reproduce or relieve your symptoms [26]. If myelopathy is suspected, the exam also includes the legs, balance, and signs such as overactive reflexes (hyperreflexia) [28]. The pattern of weak muscles (myotome) is one of the better clues to the level [32].

Imaging tests

TestWhat it showsWhy it matters for disc replacement
X-rays (incl. flexion-extension views)Bone alignment, disc-space narrowing, foraminal narrowing [26]Shows remaining motion and instability. Policies exclude more than 3–3.5 mm of slippage or more than 11° of angulation [23, 21]
MRISoft tissues: herniated discs, nerve roots, spinal cord [26]Confirms that a disc or spur is compressing the root or cord at the level that matches your symptoms [21]

Abnormal MRI does not always mean a cause

Disc degeneration is common in people with no symptoms and increases with age [37, 26]. A surgeon therefore looks for concordance: the compressed root on imaging should match your pain pattern, weakness and reflex changes.

Conditions that can mimic radiculopathy

  • Shoulder problems such as rotator cuff disease [26]
  • Nerve entrapment at the elbow (cubital tunnel) or wrist (carpal or ulnar tunnel) [26]
  • Other causes of nerve damage that EMG can help separate [26]

Tests specific to disc-replacement planning

  • Disc height and motion: severe spondylosis (disc height under 3 mm, bridging spurs, or no motion) is a contraindication in the NASS recommendation [23].
  • Facet joints: severe facet arthritis is a contraindication in FDA labeling and NASS [18, 23]. The Medicare LCD treats moderate-to-severe facet arthropathy as investigational [21].
  • Future MRI needs: implant material affects later MRI quality. Titanium produced the least artifact, cobalt-chrome obscured the treated level, and stainless steel obscured both treated and neighboring levels [39].
