Conservative (Nonsurgical) Treatment

📚 Deeper reading. This is one of the detailed research pages. You don't need it to get ready. Start with Sarah's home page and What to know.

Medical disclaimer: This private guide is general education compiled from published sources and the public website of Dr. Rovner's practice. It is not medical advice and does not replace the instructions Sarah gets from her surgical team. If anything here differs from what they say, follow them. For trouble breathing or rapidly increasing neck swelling, call 911 (see red-flag card).

Nonsurgical care is the first step for almost everyone with cervical radiculopathy. Most people improve, and insurers usually require documented conservative treatment before approving disc replacement.

Natural history: most people get better

  • "The majority of patients with cervical radiculopathy get better over time and do not need treatment," according to AAOS. Some improve within days or weeks; others take longer [26].
  • In the Rochester population study, 90% had no symptoms or only mild ones at last follow-up, and 26% had surgery [35].
  • A systematic review found that improvement often happens within 4–6 months [36].

Common nonsurgical treatments

TreatmentWhat it doesNotes
Soft cervical collarRests the neck muscles and limits motion that pinches the nerve [26]Short-term only; long-term use may weaken neck muscles [26]
Physical therapyExercises to relieve pain, strengthen and improve motion; sometimes traction [26]Aetna requires 6 weeks of in-person PT within the past year before surgery, with exceptions [22]
NSAIDsAnti-inflammatory pain relief (ibuprofen, naproxen) [26]Some trials stopped NSAIDs around surgery (Mobi-C: 1 week before to 3 months after) [11]
Oral corticosteroidsA short course to reduce swelling around the nerve [26]
Epidural / selective nerve root steroid injectionReduces local inflammation [26]Does not relieve the structural pressure, but may calm the pain long enough for the nerve to recover [26]. Some trials excluded epidural steroids within 14 days before surgery [11]
OpioidsFor severe pain not relieved by other options [26]Limited time only; risk of addiction [26]

How long before considering surgery?

FDA labels, the NASS recommendation and the Medicare LCD use at least 6 weeks of failed conservative care as the usual threshold. They make exceptions for progressive or severe neurological signs [9, 23, 21].

  • Aetna waives its PT requirement for cord compression or myelopathy, severe weakness (strength grade 4− or worse), or a progressive deficit [22].
  • The Medicare LCD requires physician-directed conservative care, including pain management, unless symptoms are severe or progressive [21].
  • NASS also counts inability to work as a reason to consider surgery after conservative care [23].

Myelopathy is different

For spinal-cord compression, nonsurgical care does not create more room for the cord. AAOS describes myelopathy as generally surgical [28]. The AO Spine guideline recommends surgery for moderate or severe myelopathy, and surgery or supervised rehabilitation for mild disease [38].

Keep records

If surgery becomes an option, insurers will ask for dates and details of PT, medications, injections and imaging. Keeping copies speeds up prior authorization [22, 21].
