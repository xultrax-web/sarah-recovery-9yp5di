What the practice says about cervical disc replacement

  • Surgery is done "through the front part of the neck," between the muscle and the swallowing and breathing tubes Stated by practice [77].
  • Swallowing: the esophagus is gently moved aside, which "can cause a week or 2 of difficulty swallowing," and only rarely lasting problems Stated by practice [77].
  • Voice: the nerve to the vocal cord can be irritated, "usually transient" Stated by practice [77].
  • Infection, nerve injury and bleeding are described as very rare Stated by practice [77].
  • Good candidates have a damaged disc pressing on a nerve root or the spinal cord, with only mild arthritis in the side (uncovertebral) and back (facet) joints Stated by practice [77].

The practice's published post-op instructions

The practice publishes general post-op instructions plus short notes for specific operations, including disc replacement Stated by practice [76]. The guidance they give for disc replacement is to follow the wound-care rules, and "since there is no bone growth expectation, resume activities as tolerated." In summary:

Disc & Spine post-op instructions (paraphrased; quotes are the practice's words)
DressingRemove it on day 2 or 3; if the wound is dry, leave it off. Some drainage is normal: use a dry gauze dressing and as little tape as possible. Antibiotic ointment isn't needed; dry blood can be gently wiped with peroxide and gauze.
ShoweringOnce the wound is sealed (3 days) and dry, shower normally and blot dry. Try not to remove the steri-strips. No baths or pools until the post-op visit.
DietNormally no restrictions. "This is not a good time to diet." Red meat and iron supplements help with post-op anemia.
ActivityBed rest is discouraged; "go as far as you can" walking. Avoid "excessive" lifting, bending, twisting, sitting or standing. Running probably not early on.
Driving"Not restricted beyond common sense and good judgement."
FeverThe page reads "Fever is normal in the first 35 days (even as high as 102+)," then says: if a fever over 101 persists, call the office during the day. "35 days" is very likely a typo for "3–5 days" Inferred. Please ask the office what fever should make you call.
MedicinesUsually a narcotic pain medicine (Tylenol may be substituted if it works), sometimes a muscle relaxant. Narcotics cause constipation, so use the minimum. Avoid NSAIDs (Advil, Aleve, etc.). No routine antibiotics after surgery.
Physical therapyGenerally not needed unless there's a specific reason.
FlyingOnce the wound is healed and no visit is needed for a while.

Their separate note for fusion (a brace is for comfort only; avoid NSAIDs for 3 months) is written for fusion patients, not disc replacement [76]. Ask which notes apply to you.

What the website doesn't publish

  • Pre-op instructions: fasting, which medicines to stop, arrival time. This guide uses general, reputable instructions instead, and labels them "confirm with your surgical team."
  • An after-hours or on-call number.
  • Where surgery is done, and which implants are currently used.
  • Patient forms for Dr. Rovner's patients are on the forms page (registration packet and follow-up questionnaire) Stated by practice [80].

Where the practice's pages differ from other sources

  • Driving: Dr. Rovner's page says common sense. Other sources say not while taking narcotic pain medicine, not before you can turn to check your blind spot [91, 93], or no driving for 2 weeks after spine surgery [88]. For you, ask the surgeon and don't drive on narcotics.
  • Fever: the practice says call if a fever over 101 persists [76]. MedlinePlus says contact the surgeon for chills or a fever of 101°F or higher [88]; a Canadian hospital booklet uses 100.4°F [91].
  • Nerve maps: the practice's page says C7 numbness travels to the ring and little fingers [77]. Merck Manual describes C7 pain going to the middle finger [30]. Real symptom maps overlap a lot [32].
